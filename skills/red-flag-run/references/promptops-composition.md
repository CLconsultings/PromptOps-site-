# PromptOps composition rules

Use these rules when Red Flag Run is applied to PromptOps product copy, licensed assets,
PromptOps documentation, or claims about authorship, provenance, IP, versioning, or deployment.

## Authority boundary

PromptOps is the governing product and licensing context. Red Flag Run is only the final
writing and authorship-preservation layer.

Do not let editing change:

- the identity of a licensed asset or its owner
- approval, deployment, certification, or version status
- licensing rights or restrictions
- proprietary workflow or framework names
- factual statements about what a system can technically verify

If the current approved PromptOps source does not support a claim, flag the gap rather than
strengthening the copy.

## Provenance is stronger than style inference

Treat direct records such as source files, revision history, explicit attribution, version
records, and approved repositories as stronger evidence of authorship than stylistic signals.

A Red Flag Run may identify formulaic or AI-associated writing patterns. It may not conclude
that AI wrote the text, certify that a human wrote it, or represent a style scan as forensic
proof of authorship.

When PromptOps uses terms such as authorship protection, fingerprinting, traceability, or
certification, describe only the mechanism that is actually supported. Separate:

- provenance records
- version traceability
- access or licensing controls
- style-pattern analysis
- actual authorship conclusions

Do not collapse those categories.

## PromptOps writing posture

Prefer language that is operational, specific, and testable. Preserve the writer's own
language when it is clear and accurate.

Avoid product copy that substitutes claims such as "trusted," "secure," "certified," or
"protected" for the mechanism that creates the condition. When the mechanism is known,
state it. When it is not known, do not invent it.

Keep the smallest responsible edit. PromptOps' concern with provenance means unnecessary
rewriting can itself reduce traceability by displacing the source author's wording.
